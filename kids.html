SKEETER SCHOOL // FOR KIDS 8–14
MOSQUITO FACTS  //  DOLAND, SOUTH DAKOTA

Meet the Mosquito

She weighs about as much as a grain of sand, she's been around since the dinosaurs, and worldwide she's the most dangerous animal alive. Here's how she works — and how you can outsmart her in your own backyard.

01

Wild Facts

Stuff most grown-ups don't know.

Mosquitoes are strange, and the strange parts are exactly what makes them beatable. Every fact below comes from real scientists — sources are at the bottom of the page.

01 Only the females bite

Male mosquitoes drink flower nectar and never bite anything. The female needs the protein in blood to make her eggs. That's the only reason she's after you.

02 She smells your breath

Every time you breathe out you puff carbon dioxide. She can pick that up from about 35 metres — a bit more than a basketball court — then zigzags toward you through the plume.

03 That whine is her wings

Some mosquitoes beat their wings up to 600 times per second. The buzz by your ear is the actual sound of wings moving way too fast to see.

04 She's a terrible flier

Top speed is about 1 to 1.5 mph — slower than you walk. That's the whole reason a plain box fan on the porch works so well. She simply can't fight the wind.

05 Her eggs float in a raft

Our local mosquito glues 100 to 300 eggs together into one floating raft about a quarter-inch long. It looks like a little fleck of soot sitting on the water.

06 Babies breathe through a snorkel

Mosquito larvae hang upside down just under the surface and breathe air through a tube on their tail end called a siphon. Poke the water and they wriggle straight down.

07 The next stage is a "tumbler"

After the larva comes the pupa, nicknamed a tumbler because it somersaults away when disturbed. It eats nothing at all for up to 48 hours while it rebuilds itself into an adult.

08 Egg to adult in about a week

The whole transformation takes 7 to 10 days in warm water. Forget a bucket for two weeks and you've raised a whole generation without meaning to.

09 Deadliest animal on Earth

Mosquito-borne disease kills an estimated 725,000+ people a year worldwide — more than sharks, snakes, and lions put together. Most of that is malaria in the tropics, spread by a different mosquito than ours.

10 They watched the dinosaurs

The oldest mosquito ever found is stuck in amber about 99 million years old. Mosquitoes were already biting things while dinosaurs were still walking around.

11 A bottle cap is enough

She doesn't need a pond or a swamp. A capful of water sitting still for about a week is a working mosquito nursery. That's why the hunt below matters.

02

Egg to Bite in 10 Days

Four stages, three of them underwater.

A mosquito doesn't grow up the way you do — it completely rebuilds itself four times. Here's the whole life, start to finish:

1. Egg

1–10 days. Mom lays a raft of 100–300 eggs on the surface of still water. Warmer water, faster hatch.

2. Larva

6–8 days. The "wiggler." Hangs under the surface breathing through its siphon, eating gunk in the water, and shedding its skin four times as it grows.

3. Pupa

Up to 48 hours. The "tumbler." Eats nothing, just floats near the top and rebuilds into an adult inside its own shell.

4. Adult

Flies away. The adult climbs out onto the surface, dries its wings, and takes off. Females go looking for blood, and the whole thing starts over.

Notice something? Three of the four stages happen in standing water. No water, no mosquitoes — that's the entire trick.
03

Why We Care in Doland

The honest version.
WEST NILE VIRUS — THE SHORT VERSION

What it is, without the scary movie music

Some mosquitoes carry a germ called West Nile virus. You can only get it from a mosquito bite — you can't catch it from a friend, a pet, or a doorknob.

Here's the honest math: about 4 out of 5 people who get infected never feel sick at all and never even know. About 1 in 5 get something like the flu — fever, headache, achy. A small number get seriously ill and have to go to the hospital.

There's no vaccine for people and no medicine that cures it, so the only real defense is fewer mosquitoes and fewer bites. South Dakota gets more West Nile than most states, which is why people around here take a bucket of old water more seriously than you'd expect.

The mosquito doing most of the carrying here is called Culex tarsalis. She's active mostly at dusk and after dark — which is a useful thing to know when you're deciding whether to grab the bug spray before a summer evening outside.

04

Your Mission: The Bottle-Cap Hunt

You're better at this than adults are.

Grown-ups walk past standing water all summer without seeing it. Kids are shorter, nosier, and better at looking under things — which makes you genuinely good at this job.

▸ THE HUNT — SIX STEPS
  • Bring a grown-up. You're the spotter. They handle anything that needs lifting, dumping, or treating.
  • Go the day after it rains. That's when hidden water shows up in places nobody thinks about.
  • Hunt for still water. Anything holding water for more than a few days counts. Check the list below.
  • Tip it and toss it. Dump the water out, then flip the container upside down so it can't fill up again.
  • Report what you can't dump. Stock tanks, ponds, clogged gutters, big barrels — point those out and let an adult deal with them.
  • Do it again next week. Mosquitoes need about a week to grow up. Beat them every week and they never finish.
WHERE TO LOOK

Water hides in boring places. These are the ones people miss most:

  • Buckets & pails
  • Old tires
  • Kiddie pools
  • Toys left outside
  • Flowerpot saucers
  • Birdbaths
  • Pet water bowls
  • Wheelbarrows
  • Tarps & their folds
  • Clogged gutters
  • Trash-can lids
  • Puddles that never dry
⚠ MISSION RULES — READ BEFORE YOU START

1. Never go near creeks, sloughs, dugouts, or ponds without an adult. Open water is dangerous no matter how shallow it looks.

2. Don't drink, splash in, or play in trap water. The water in a mosquito trap is gross on purpose. Leave it alone.

3. Mosquito dunks are a grown-up job. They're safe when used right, but you don't handle them — an adult does.

4. Don't touch or move anyone else's traps, and stay off other people's property unless you've been invited.

5. Wear bug spray at dusk — but let an adult put it on you and read the label first.

05

Check Yourself

Tap a question to see the answer.

Read the page already? Prove it. (No grades, no permanent record.)

Do all mosquitoes bite people?

No — only the females. Males spend their whole lives drinking nectar from flowers. A female bites because she needs the protein in blood to produce her eggs.

How long does it take an egg to become a flying adult?

About 7 to 10 days in warm water. That's why dumping standing water once a week works so well — you break the cycle before anything can finish growing up.

How much water does a mosquito need to breed?

Roughly a bottle cap's worth, standing still for about a week. She does not need a pond, a creek, or a swamp — a forgotten toy in the yard will do fine.

Why does a box fan on the porch keep mosquitoes off you?

Because they're weak fliers — only about 1 to 1.5 mph. Moving air is faster than they are, so they simply can't get to you through it.

Three of the four life stages happen where?

In standing water. Egg, larva, and pupa are all aquatic — only the adult flies. That single fact is the reason getting rid of standing water beats every gadget ever invented.

Are mosquitoes really the deadliest animal on Earth?

Yes, by a wide margin — mosquito-borne diseases kill an estimated 725,000 or more people worldwide every year. Most of those deaths are from malaria in tropical countries, carried by a different kind of mosquito than the one we have here. Our local concern is West Nile virus.

What's the single best thing you can do about mosquitoes?

Get rid of standing water. Not a zapper, not a gadget, not a bat house — just dumping the water where they breed. It's free, it takes fifteen minutes, and it beats everything else.
